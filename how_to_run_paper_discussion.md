As for outline of how to run a paper discussion class - 

Pre/start of class - Have them write on the right wall terms they didn’t understand, on the left questions they were left with at the end
Talk through any terms that they don’t know that will be helpful in discussion. If you’d like, show some pictures of the system to give them context. Take no more than 10 minutes for this! Hopefully less!
For 30 minutes, divide them into the following groups (I sometimes even make a slide with this on it so they can refer back)
Introduction - Why is this paper important - what is the context? What is their central idea they are pursuing?
Methods - What exactly did they do? Where did they do it? Why is this a good test of their central idea? Or is it?
Results - What did they find? Be able to explain each figure and what it tells us. What do the results mean given their question?
Discussion - Sum up their results. Put them in context of big ideas that exist in the literature.
For 30 minutes, recombine into “paper” groups. Each group should have 1 person per section (sometimes one group will have 2 due to uneven numbers.) Each group should
Walk through the paper, from intro to discussion with one member from each group presenting to the rest of their group.
Answer the question - do you believe it, or were there flaws in their methods or logic that might mean their answer is wrong?
Answer the question - if there are flaws, does it REALLY change their conclusions or not?
Answer the question - what new thing does this teach us about the world?
Answer the question - where would you go from here, and what does this suggest as something we should explore next?
Come back together as a class, and have a larger discussion of what does this paper mean and do they believe it. Let them raise things they thought were interesting or things they thought were problematic and talk it through.

Throughout, when you divide them into groups, give them 5 minutes to talk themselves - although stuck your head in if nothing is happening. I usually get some water then or some such to keep myself from jumping in. Then, make sure you circulate to each group, answering questions (this is usually particularly important for the methods group) and making sure they know what they are talking about. During the second part, play a little devil’s advocate - make sure they know what the paper says, but, push them on some of the questions I outlined above. Save up things you find notable for the end of class discussion.

Note - feel free to make slides of anything you think will be helpful. For papers in the past, I’ve had to explain thing like organismal identity or function, shown pictures of systems or experimental setups, explained (sometimes with a slide, sometimes just drawing on the wall) p-values, ANOVA, nMDS. Do what you think will best prepare them.
