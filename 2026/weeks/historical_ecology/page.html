🗓️ Week 06 - Historical Ecology

🌊 🦀 Tides for this week

Note: No Class on Monday for Indigenous People’s Day

In this week, we will learn to think in terms of historical and deep time in order to understand present-day ocean ecosystems. How have ecosystems changed over the entirety of human history? What can we learn from historical records that teach us about how ecosystems function - perhaps even with less human impacts than we see today.

🧠🐛❓ Would you like to know more?

The OG reference that made a lot of us sit up and realize we neede to think about historical context, see Steneck and Carlton (2001) - Human alteration of marine communities: Students Beware!

For more on perception versus reality with respect to whales and cod as a great example of shifting baselines, see McClenachan and Neal (2023)

To see how ecology, interviews, and historical records can be integrated on one place, see one of my favorite papers ever Salomon, Tanape, and Huntington (2007).

For the classic reference in shifting baselines in kelp forests systems, see Dayton et al. (1998).

Let’s bring a deeper time perspective to thinking about ecosystem collapse with Jackson et al. (2001) and follow it up with Lotze et al. (2006).

References
