🗓️ Week 03 - Marine Biogeography and Oceanography

🌊 🦀 Tides for this week

Note: No class on Monday due to Yom Kippur.

In this week, we will work to understand how and why are organisms distributed across the ocean. We will look at major current systems and how they influence the biogeography of life in the sea with a focus on the Gulf of Maine. We will examine the role of dispersal in regulating biogeography in the face of these currents, as well as how organisms cope with being something other than passive particles. Finally, we will discussthe role of current and historic abiotic conditions driving macroscale patterns life at sea.

🧠🐛❓ Would you like to know more?

For a good review of the early history of supply-side ecology, see Grosberg and Levitan (1992).

For more on global biogeography and its relationship to dispersal, see Álvarez-Noriega et al. (2020)

To understand the paleo-perspective on biogeography, see Jablonski and Sepkoski Jr. (1996), a classic.

To really get into the local adaptation side of marine invertebrates and how that relates to dispersal, see this excellent review by Sanford and Kelly (2011)

To learn more about currents in the Gulf of Maine and kelp dispersal biogeography, see Francolini et al. (2026)

The Cradle, Museum, Out of the Tropics model we discussed this week comes from Jablonski et al. 2006

The working group papser on the biogeography of extinction and risk comes from Finnegan et al. (2015) - the first time your prof was ever called a “Neoecologist”.
